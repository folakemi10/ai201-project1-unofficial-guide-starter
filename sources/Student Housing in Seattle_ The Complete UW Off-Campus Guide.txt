Seattle's student housing market moves fast — and for University of Washington students, understanding how it
works is the difference between landing a great apartment and settling for whatever's left in August. The
neighborhoods around UW offer everything from classic U-District walk-ups to modern Capitol Hill apartments
connected by light rail. This guide covers everything UW students need to know about finding student housing in
Seattle, from where to search to what to prioritize in a city known for rain, hills, and rising rents.

The U-District: UW's Home Base for Student Housing
The University District is where the majority of UW students live off campus — and for good reason. It's walkable to
campus, packed with student-oriented services, and offers housing at nearly every price point. The Ave (University
Way NE) is the neighborhood's commercial spine, lined with restaurants, coffee shops, and bookstores.

For quieter living within the U-District, look for apartments on streets east of 15th Ave NE or north of NE 50th Street.
New developments near the U-District light rail station have added modern options, though at higher price points
than older buildings.

Beyond the U-District: Affordable Alternatives
Ravenna, Wallingford, and Roosevelt offer UW students more space and often lower rent than the U-District core.
Ravenna is quiet and leafy with excellent bike access to campus. Wallingford has a charming neighborhood feel and
Gas Works Park. Roosevelt gained a Link Light Rail station that makes the UW commute fast and reliable. Capitol Hill
is further but offers Seattle's most vibrant urban experience with a quick light rail ride to campus. Each neighborhood
has a distinct personality — visiting in person before committing is essential.

What to Prioritize in Seattle Student Housing
Seattle's climate and geography create specific housing priorities that students from other cities might not consider.
Covered or indoor bike storage is essential — bikes left outside corrode quickly in Seattle's damp air. In-unit or inbuilding laundry matters more here than in dry climates because hanging clothes to dry is impractical much of the
year. Good insulation and efficient heating keep winter bills manageable. Proximity to a bus stop or light rail station is
more valuable than parking for most UW students. And natural light is a genuine quality-of-life factor during Seattle's
dark winter months — prioritize south-facing windows when possible.

Timing and Strategy for UW Housing Searches
The UW rental market peaks from May through August for September move-ins. Starting your search in March or
April gives you the widest selection and most landlord flexibility. Winter searches (November through February) often
yield lower rents and motivated landlords willing to negotiate on terms. Use OCUniverse, Zillow, Apartments.com,
and UW's off-campus housing resources simultaneously. Set up alerts so you're notified immediately when new units
matching your criteria are listed — in the U-District, good units get snatched within days.
